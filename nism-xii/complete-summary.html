Understanding Securities Markets and Performance

≈10 marks · 16 subtopics · chapter priority ★★★★★

Financial market & securities market

★★★★★
  • Financial market = money, debt, equity markets + investors (buyers) + issuers (users of funds) + intermediaries + regulators (SEBI, RBI…)
  • Securities market = regulated framework for flow of capital (equity & debt) from investors to businesses; channels savings into investments
  • Securities = financial instruments issued by companies, FIs or government to raise funds; investors turn savings into financial assets

Features of securities

★★★★★
  • Represent terms of exchange of money between two parties; issuer and investor goals are complementary
  • Issuer sets terms; listing makes security liquid and gives information to investors
  • Investor gets rights: ownership, participation in management or claim on assets
  • Broad classes: equity and debt; return = benefit; risk = chance return does not come (bond: default on interest or principal)
  • Market lets investors take risk for return, judge it with information, and transfer it by selling

Investors: retail vs institutional

★★★★★
  • Retail = individuals on personal account (A invests ₹5,000/month in MF units)
  • Institutional = companies, banks, govt bodies, MFs, insurers, pension funds, endowments, societies (ABC MF buys 50,000 XYZ shares)
  • MFs are institutional investors 'by objective'; banks/FIs invest via treasury
  • Institutions run formal processes: select securities · review & monitor · manage risk, return, holding period

SEBI's functions

★★★★★
  • Recognises and regulates stock exchanges; administers SCRA 1956; Central Govt may nominate up to 3 persons on each exchange; exchanges submit bye-laws for approval, periodic and daily monitoring reports
  • Codified regulations for all activities and intermediaries
  • Primary market: SEBI (ICDR) Regulations 2018 — public & rights issues, QIP, preferential, bonus, IDRs; debt: SEBI (NCS) Regulations 2021 (9 Aug 2021, merged Debt Securities Regs 2008 and NCRPS Regs 2013)
  • Listing agreement: continuous information flow, governance, investor protection
  • Inspections; investigations into manipulation, artificial volume, insider trading, takeover code, issue malpractice; surveillance at exchanges
  • Powers: call for information, summon, examine witnesses, search & seizure; penalties = suspension, monetary penalty, prosecution
  • Insider trading code: compliance officer, insider disclosures, confidentiality; penalties incl. prosecution, market ban, voiding trades

Types of equity: DVR & preference shares

★★★★★
  • DVR shares: separate profit right from vote — usually higher dividend, fewer votes; promoters raise capital without losing control
  • Preference shares: fixed dividend; paid after all other payments but before equity dividend; priority over equity in dividend and in capital on winding up; no voting, no claim on assets
  • Cumulative preference: unpaid dividend carried forward if terms allow
  • Usually unlisted, little capital appreciation — value not tied to company performance

Equity risk & return

★★★★★
  • Return = dividend + price appreciation; depends on future residual cash flows and price paid
  • Risk: benefits not assured; prices 'noisy' as many players react to news
  • Prices mirror performance — barometer of company, sector, economy

Zero sum, settlement, OTC vs exchange, arbitrage

★★★★★
  • Every long has a matching short → net economic value of all positions = zero; no new asset created
  • Cash settlement = pay price difference, no delivery
  • Since October 2019: all stock F&O open at expiry settled by physical delivery; index F&O stay cash-settled
  • OTC: mutually agreed, non-standard, rely on trust, between institutions; exchange-traded: standard, via clearing corporation with margins, anonymous
  • Forwards = OTC; futures = exchange-traded
  • Law of one price: identical goods can differ across markets only by transaction costs (transport, storage, insurance, interest); arbitrageurs close gaps
  • Derivative price differs from cash price by costs such as warehousing and interest

★★★★★

  • A sells to B (OI 1); A later buys from C to square off → A out, but B short vs C long → OI unchanged

PCR = put options outstanding ÷ call options outstanding
More calls → PCR < 1 ; more puts → PCR > 1
Traders read PCR as bullish or bearish in different ways
