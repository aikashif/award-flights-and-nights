Concept & Role of a Mutual Fund

≈4 marks · 9 subtopics · chapter priority ★★★★★

What a mutual fund is

★★★★★
DefinitionPool of money from many investors, invested per a stated objective, managed professionally
Legal formTrust (SEBI-registered)
UnitsInvestor gets units; ownership ∝ units held
Face valueUsually ₹10 per unit at NFO
NAVValue per unit = net assets ÷ units
AUMTotal market value of scheme assets = NAV × units
Pass-throughGains/losses belong to unitholders; AMC only charges fees

Key terms: scheme, plan, option

★★★★★
TermMeaning
SchemeThe fund (e.g. ABC Flexi Cap Fund)
PlanDirect (no distributor, lower TER) / Regular (via distributor)
OptionGrowth (no payout) / IDCW payout / IDCW reinvestment / IDCW transfer
IDCWIncome Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal (renamed from 'dividend', 2021) — paid from investor's own capital/gains
Trap Direct plan NAV > regular plan NAV of the same scheme (lower expenses), never the opposite.

Advantages of mutual funds

★★★★★
AdvantageNote
Professional managementResearch, fund manager
DiversificationMany securities with small money
Economies of scaleLower transaction cost per rupee
LiquidityOpen-ended: redeem any business day
Tax deferral / efficiencyTax only on redemption; no tax inside fund
ConvenienceSIP, STP, SWP, switch, online
Low ticket sizeSIP from ₹100–500
TransparencyDaily NAV, monthly portfolio
Regulatory comfortSEBI regulated

Limitations of mutual funds

★★★★★
  • No customisation of portfolio
  • Choice overload (many schemes)
  • No control over costs / fund manager decisions
  • No guaranteed returns
  • Expenses reduce returns
  • Applicable NAV known only after cut-off

Legal Structure of Mutual Funds in India

≈3 marks · 7 subtopics · chapter priority ★★★★★

Three-tier structure

★★★★★
SPONSOR  (holds ≥40% of AMC net worth)
   │ sets up trust, signs trust deed
   ▼
TRUSTEES  (hold assets for unitholders)
   │ appoint AMC via Investment Management Agreement
   ▼
AMC  (manages the money)
   │ appoints
   ▼
Custodian · RTA · Fund accountant · Auditor · Bankers · Distributors

INVESTORS = beneficial owners of scheme assets
Trust deed: sponsor + trusteesInvestment Management Agreement (IMA): trustees + AMCRegistered under Indian Registration Act 1908 / Indian Trusts Act 1882

Sponsor

★★★★★
RolePromoter; sets up trust and AMC; like a company's promoter
Eligibility (traditional)5 yrs in financial services · positive net worth all 5 yrs · net worth (last yr) > contribution to AMC · profit in 3 of last 5 yrs incl. latest
Stake≥40% of AMC net worth (anyone holding ≥40% is deemed sponsor)
Alternative routePrivate equity / other sponsors allowed with conditions (2023) ‡
LiabilityNot liable for scheme losses beyond initial contribution

Trustees

★★★★★
FormBoard of trustees or trustee company
Number≥4 trustees; ≥2/3 independent (not associated with sponsor)
RoleGuardians of unitholders' interest; supervise AMC
DutiesApprove schemes · ensure AMC compliance · check AMC transactions with associates · review investor complaints · report to SEBI half-yearly
MeetingsAt least 6 a year (once every 2 months)
PowersCan remove AMC (with unitholder approval / SEBI); approve key personnel
Not allowedTrustee of one MF can't be trustee of another (unless independent in both, with approval)

Asset Management Company (AMC)

★★★★★
RoleManages the money; launches schemes; investment decisions
Net worth≥ ₹50 crore (all times)
Board≥50% independent directors
ApprovalSEBI-approved; appointed by trustees
BusinessOnly MF management + permitted management/advisory services to pooled funds (PMS/AIF via conditions), no conflicting business
Key peopleCEO, CIO, fund managers, compliance officer, investor relations officer
Skin in the gameAMC invests a set % of AUM in its schemes; part of key employees' pay in scheme units (lock-in) ‡
LiabilityActs as agent; trust owns assets — not AMC

Service providers

★★★★★
EntityRoleKey rule
CustodianSafekeeps securities, settles trades, corporate actionsSEBI-registered; independent of sponsor (sponsor/associates hold <50% of custodian's voting rights, or 50% independent directors)
RTAInvestor records: purchase, redemption, statementsSEBI-registered (e.g. CAMS, KFintech)
Fund accountantCalculates NAVCan be AMC in-house or outsourced
AuditorAudits scheme accountsDifferent auditors for AMC and schemes
Collecting / paying bankersReceive and pay money
DistributorsSell unitsARN holders (AMFI)
KRAKYC recordsCVL, NDML, CAMS KRA, KFin KRA, DotEx

Role of regulators

★★★★★
Regulator / bodyRole
SEBIPrimary regulator; SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations 1996; registration, investment rules, disclosure
RBIBank-sponsored MFs (as bank regulator); money market & debt market rules; overseas investment limits
Ministry of FinancePolicy, taxation
Ministry of Corporate Affairs / ROCAMC and trustee company are companies
Stock exchangesListing of close-ended / ETFs; MF platforms (NSE NMF II, BSE StAR MF)

AMFI

★★★★★
Set up1995 (non-profit industry association)
Is AMFI an SRO?No — trade body; SEBI regulates
DoesARN registration of distributors · AMFI Code of Ethics (ACE) for AMCs · Code of Conduct for intermediaries · investor awareness ('Mutual Funds Sahi Hai') · publishes NAVs · large/mid/small cap list
ObjectivesProfessional & ethical standards, represent industry to SEBI/Govt, investor education

Key investment restrictions

★★★★★
LimitRule ‡

AMFI Code of Conduct for intermediaries

★★★★★
  • Risk-profile the client; recommend suitable products
  • Disclose all commissions (trail etc.) from competing schemes
  • Don't rebate commission or give gifts to induce
  • No churning (switching for commission)
  • Don't misrepresent / promise returns
  • Keep client information confidential
  • Ensure KYC; don't handle investor cash beyond rules
  • Keep ARN valid; follow SEBI & AMFI circulars

Due diligence of distributors by AMCs

★★★★★

AMCs must do due diligence on distributors meeting ANY of:

  • Multiple points of presence (>20 locations)
  • AUM > ₹100 crore (non-institutional) across industry
  • Commission > ₹1 crore p.a. across industry
  • Commission > ₹50 lakh from a single MF
