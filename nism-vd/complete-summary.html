Factors to evaluate investments

★★★★★

Three most important: safety, liquidity, returns. Plus convenience, ticket size, taxability, tax deduction.

FactorWorkbook points
SafetySafety of capital + degree of surety of income; understand the risks
LiquidityEase of converting to cash; product nature or features (lock-in, exit penalty). Penalty doesn't hamper liquidity — only lowers return. Divisibility: can you sell part?
ReturnsCurrent income (periodic, without selling) + capital appreciation (only on sale). Exit charges = liquidity vs return trade-off
ConvenienceEase of investing, withdrawing (full/part), checking value, receiving income
Ticket sizeMin amount: from ₹50/₹100 to >₹1 lakh or >₹1 crore; don't choose just because affordable
Taxability of incomePost-tax return matters; don't view tax in isolation; some products tax-efficient only if held to maturity/min period
Tax deductionRaises effective return (on net amount invested); often comes with lock-in → liquidity vs deduction trade-off
Trap No factor in isolation; consider the investor's situation.

Investment risks

★★★★★
RiskWorkbook points
Inflation riskErodes purchasing power; hits hard over long periods. Nominal rate = return without inflation; real rate = inflation-adjusted. Safety + anytime liquidity usually earns below inflation (e.g. FD 7% vs inflation 8%)
Liquidity riskPromised return only if held to maturity (FD premature penalty; PPF no/partial liquidity); real estate takes weeks/months; listed equity liquid but volatile → unsuitable for short-term needs
Credit riskDelay or default of interest/principal. 3 outcomes: pays on time / pays late / doesn't pay. Causes: ability (business stability, profitability) or intention. Own-country government bonds safest → lowest rates
Market & price riskVolatility from opinion changes. Market-wide (e.g. war fear) vs company-specific (product sales fall) vs industry-specific (policy, technology — pager industry vanished). Specific risk reduced by diversification; market-wide not
Interest rate riskValue changes with rates; affects bonds more than stocks; inverse relation; longer maturity → bigger price swings; market-wide

Role of mutual funds

★★★★★

  • 'Fund' and 'scheme' used interchangeably; the offering entity = 'mutual fund' / 'the fund'

  • Livelihood for MF employees, distributors, registrars, service providers

  • Risks: can't liquidate till recovery; may realise nothing; listing ≠ liquidity; may trade well below NAV

Mutual Fund Scheme Selection

≈10 marks · 17 subtopics · chapter priority ★★★★★

Selection based on investor need, risk profile, allocation

★★★★★
  • Need: long-term appreciation, periodic income, or parking with high liquidity
  • Portfolio = means to an end (goal) — never lose sight of the goal
  • Step 1: set financial goals; Step 2: understand situation and risk appetite
  • Risk appetite = need + ability + willingness to take risk (SEBI due-diligence circular and AMFI code stress risk profiling)
  • Asset allocation decided after risk profile, goals, situation: growth → equity (needs long horizon); income → debt
  • Long horizon but unwilling to take risk → may prefer debt
  • Age alone is not a good basis — goals and situations differ even within an age group; family goals
  • Use time horizon to the goal: longer horizon → more risk capacity; liquidity may be needed even in long-term portfolios

Active vs passive funds

Diversified vs focused vs sector vs thematic

★★★★★

  • Focused: concentrated in few stocks → riskier than diversified, less risky than sector
  • Sector (pharma, banking): concentration risk; needs skill to time entry before up-cycle and exit before downturn → satellite portfolio

  • Thematic (MNC, PSU, infrastructure): between diversified and sector

Large vs mid vs small cap; multi/flexi

★★★★★
  • Large caps: established, stable revenues/profits, withstand downturns
  • Mid/small caps: early growth; boom in good times, many fall by the wayside in turmoil; attractive when front-line stocks get expensive; winners re-rated as they grow
  • Need careful selection — risky to put a large share of portfolio there
  • Multi cap: spread across caps, lower risk than mid/small; large & mid: riskier than large, less than mid; flexi cap: manager's freedom
  • Choose multi cap (similar allocation), flexi cap (manager decides) or separate large/mid/small funds (investor decides)
