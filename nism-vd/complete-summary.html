★★★★★

★★★★★

Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)

★★★★★
  • Redeem a constant value periodically — avoid exiting all at a trough
  • ₹1,000/month: NAV 10 → 100 units; NAV 12 → 83.333; NAV 9 → 111.111
  • Exit load raises units needed: 1% load at NAV 10 → effective ₹9.90 → 101.0101 units for ₹1,000
  • Register amount, periodicity (usually monthly), period; some allow only appreciation or IDCW to be withdrawn (variable SWP — capital not withdrawn)
  • Reasons: avoid trough risk; regular expenses (retirement); regularly encash profits
  • Exit loads and taxes apply

Systematic Transfer Plan (STP)

★★★★★
  • SWP from source scheme + SIP into target scheme of the same MF
  • One transfer = switch; multiple pre-defined tranches = STP
  • Transfer = redemption → exit loads & taxes apply
  • Scheme minimum investment/redemption limits do NOT apply to STP
  • MF defines eligible source and target schemes
  • Avoids idle funds & double paperwork; used for phased equity entry, rebalancing, profit booking; fixed amount or appreciation
  • SIP, SWP, STP may be offered for SIF strategies (with minimum investment threshold)

★★★★★

Operational aspects — SIP registration

★★★★★
  • MF specifies schemes, minimum per tranche, dates, minimum tenure; investor picks amount, frequency (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual), period, dates; defaults if not chosen
  • Minor: SIP registered only till majority
  • New investor: application form + SIP enrolment form (must tally); existing: only enrolment form with folio no.; signed per mode of holding (all holders if joint)
  • Enrolment typically 15–30 days before first instalment (e.g. submitted 5 Sep, SIP date 15th, 30 days needed → first on 15 Oct); shorter if OTM registered

Gating — restriction on redemption

★★★★★
  • Only for systemic crises affecting almost all securities, not issuer-specific problems
  • Triggers: (a) market-wide liquidity issues — not to manage a scheme's own liquidity or a bad investment (b) market failure / exchange closure (political, economic, military, monetary emergencies) (c) operational issues — force majeure, technical failure (e.g. blackout) despite diligence
  • Max 10 working days in any 90-day period
  • Needs approval of AMC Board and Trustees; SEBI informed immediately
  • Requests up to ₹2 lakh not restricted; above ₹2 lakh → first ₹2 lakh paid, excess restricted
  • Possibility disclosed prominently in scheme documents

★★★★★

DefinitionsSegregated portfolio = debt/money market instrument hit by credit event, separated; main portfolio = rest
TriggerCredit event at issuer level = downgrade by a SEBI-registered CRA; optional, AMC's discretion

Segregated portfolio: TER, NAV, risks

★★★★★

  • Costs never charged to main portfolio
  • NAV declared daily; disclosed in scheme documents, monthly/half-yearly portfolios, annual report
  • Risks: can't liquidate till recovery; may realise nothing; listing ≠ liquidity; may trade well below NAV

Selection based on investor need, risk profile, allocation

★★★★★
  • Need: long-term appreciation, periodic income, or parking with high liquidity
  • Portfolio = means to an end (goal) — never lose sight of the goal
  • Step 1: set financial goals; Step 2: understand situation and risk appetite
  • Risk appetite = need + ability + willingness to take risk (SEBI due-diligence circular and AMFI code stress risk profiling)
  • Asset allocation decided after risk profile, goals, situation: growth → equity (needs long horizon); income → debt
  • Long horizon but unwilling to take risk → may prefer debt
  • Age alone is not a good basis — goals and situations differ even within an age group; family goals
  • Use time horizon to the goal: longer horizon → more risk capacity; liquidity may be needed even in long-term portfolios

Impact cost & bid-ask spread

★★★★★

Actual = (1,000 × 9.90 + 500 × 10.00) ÷ 1,500 = 9.9333
Impact cost = (9.9333 − 9.85) ÷ 9.85 × 100 = 0.84%
Trap Impact cost is HIGH when liquidity is poor.

Forward contracts

★★★★★
DefinitionAgreement directly between two parties to buy/sell an asset on a future date at terms fixed today (commodities, FX, equity, interest rates)

Cost of carry (no-arbitrage) model

★★★★★

Transaction costs & no-arbitrage bounds

  • Different participants have different costs → different fair values and bounds → continuous trading

★★★★★

Convenience yield

★★★★★
  • Intangible inflow — comfort of holding the asset (e.g. hoarding grains, heating oil during floods)
  • Subjective, differs by person and over time, hard to price
  • If convenience yield dominates cost of carry → futures at discount to spot; reverse arbitrage impossible (no one lends the asset) → model breaks down
  • Arises with scarcity / low inventory — consumption assets (commodities), not financial assets

Expectations model

★★★★★

  • Futures price = expected future spot price; futures may trade at premium or discount
  • Futures > spot → expected rise → 'contango market'; futures < spot → expected fall → 'backwardation market'

What an option is

★★★★★
  • Futures = linear payoff, unlimited loss/profit; options let you ride upside while restricting downside
  • Option = right, not obligation, to buy/sell the underlying on or before a stated date at a predetermined price
  • Right bought for the option premium, paid upfront by buyer (holder, long) to seller (writer, short)
  • Buyer exercises only when favourable; writer legally bound if exercised
  • Call = right to buy; Put = right to sell

Implied volatility

★★★★★
  • Historical volatility: SD of past % price changes (e.g. weekly Nifty changes over a year)
  • Traders want expected volatility over the option's remaining life
  • Pharma example: historical 15%, court verdict next week → expected 70–80%
  • Implied volatility = volatility backed out by running Black-Scholes in reverse from the market premium (index 17,562, 17,500 call, 9 days, ₹95, rate 6%)
  • High IV → traders sell options (premiums rich); low IV → buy options
  • Writers demand higher premiums as safety margin for asymmetric payoff; if risk priced in, even adverse verdict may not hurt writers
